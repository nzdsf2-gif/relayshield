## Q1a. StoreBot: submitted twice, no confirmation, not findable

**Do not submit a third time. When the same instruction fails twice the instruction is
the loop, and a third identical attempt produces a third identical silence.**

There are three causes and they have three different fixes:

1. **A moderation queue with no receipt.** Common on small catalogues. The listing
   appears in days and nothing is wrong.
2. **The submission never posted.** miniTelegram did exactly this to us on 2026-09-14 --
   their page crashed on the icon upload and discarded the filled form, four times. A
   crash that destroys a session looks identical to a submission that was ignored.
3. **Listed but not indexed yet**, so their search cannot find it while the page exists.

**The observation that separates them costs nothing: search their site for the bot's
exact URL path rather than for "RelayShield".** A catalogue's search usually covers
titles and descriptions, not usernames, and a listing that exists on a URL but not in
search is cause 3 and needs no action at all. If storebot.me has a `/bots/<username>`
URL pattern, try `storebot.me/bots/relayshield_bot` directly.

**Then email them once.** Their FAQ names a contact. One message, naming the bot, the
date, and that no confirmation was shown -- that is the same move that is open with
miniTelegram, and it is cheaper than any further guessing.

**THE PART THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE LISTING: the link you submitted almost certainly
did not carry `?start=SRC_storebot`.** If the listing is live and points at a bare
`t.me/relayshield_bot`, its arrivals are logged as ordinary `/start` traffic and are
indistinguishable from organic forever. There is no unmatched row to find later. When you
correct the submission, correct the link at the same time.

**UNVERIFIED from the container:** storebot.me is egress-blocked here, so I have not seen
their form, their FAQ or their URL scheme. That is a fact about this container, not about
the site.

## Q1c. WhatsApp discovery

**The front door was not created.** No `wa.me` string exists in any `.py`, `.js` or
`.json` in the repo, and `git log --all -S "wa.me"` finds no commit on any branch;
`origin/main` was identical to this branch when I checked. **UNVERIFIED for an unpushed
commit on your Mac** -- `git --no-pager log --oneline origin/main..main` settles that in

the code was not.

**But building it now would be the wrong move anyway, and this is the finding rather than
the omission.** `relayshield_whatsapp_webhook.py:5516` -- an unknown number gets *"It
looks like your account isn't set up yet. Visit relayshield.net to sign up"* and the
handler returns. The only account-creating write in that file is an employee record made
by an admin who already has one. **There is no self-serve path into the WhatsApp bot.**

So every discovery link we could place would deliver a stranger to a closed door. That is
worse than no link: it spends the impression and teaches the person the product does not
work.

**Why Telegram does not have this problem**, since the contrast is the whole answer: a
stranger can `/start` the bot, run keyless checks through the widget, use inline mode
inside a group chat they were already in, and open the Mini App -- none of it needs an
account. WhatsApp has none of those four.

**And there is no WhatsApp catalogue ecosystem to submit to, structurally.** Telegram has
a public username namespace and deep links, so third parties can build directories on top
of it. WhatsApp has neither, Meta runs no bot directory, and Click-to-WhatsApp is paid
advertising. **So WhatsApp discovery cannot be registered for. It has to be donated from
surfaces we already own** -- the developers page, the blog footer, the Mini App, the
Telegram bot, the `checkemail@` reply -- none of which mentions WhatsApp today.

**Recommended, and it is a sequence rather than a choice:**

1. **Open the door.** A keyless first-contact path for unknown numbers: the same
   `/v1/link-check` and `/v1/wallet-risk` the Telegram widget calls, both keyless and
   capped per source. Roughly a day. **This is the whole unlock** -- it turns the bot from
   a customer-only channel into something a stranger can use, which is what makes any
   link worth placing.
2. **Then the link and its attribution**, half a day: `wa.me/<number>?text=RS%20SRC_<key>`
   with the same `SRC_` parsing the Telegram webhook has, so the arrival is countable.
   The number comes from `TWILIO_WHATSAPP_NUMBER` in Secrets Manager -- I have not
   hardcoded it and no session should, because it is a live identifier in a public repo.
3. **Then place it** on the five surfaces above, one key each.

**Do not reverse 1 and 2.** Building the measurement first gives you a funnel that
measurably works and delivers nothing.

## Q2. Apify: no, do not ignore it -- but the action is a read, not an upgrade

**What the message is.** "Maximum platform usage per month" is a hard spending limit on
the account. Apify's own documentation is explicit that when usage exceeds it, **platform
services are suspended** rather than billed on. Yours is a CUSTOM limit, meaning it was
set above (or apart from) the plan default, and it expires in six days -- at which point
the limit reverts.

**So the question is not "should I upgrade". It is two numbers:**

    console.apify.com/settings/limits

Read (a) this month's usage so far, and (b) what the limit reverts to when the custom one
expires. **If (a) is comfortably below (b), ignore the email entirely.** If (a) is at or
near (b), the Actor stops when the limit lapses.

**What breaks if it does, and this is why it is not purely cosmetic.** The Actor is one of
our five MCP surfaces and it runs in **Standby**, which is kept warm and billed for active
time -- so it is the most likely thing consuming the allowance in the first place. If it
suspends:

- `_APIFY_BANNER` on the developers page claims *"The RelayShield actor runs breach,
  infostealer and SIM-swap checks as an Apify task"*. That becomes false on a live page.
- The SDK-grid card links a Store listing whose Actor cannot run.
- The Apify writers-programme article rests on the Actor being live, and its whole premise
  is that we built and ran the thing.

**What does NOT break, which is the reassuring half:** the AWS Marketplace Bundle D entity
registers the `-aws` HuggingFace Space as its MCP endpoint, and Smithery points at the
public Space. **No published product declares the Apify Actor as its endpoint**, so a
suspension does not take down a paid listing.

**And nothing watches it.** `tools/check_hf_space.py` probes both Spaces every six hours
and opens an issue on a failure; there is no equivalent for the Actor, so it would go dark
silently and we would learn about it from a prospect. Worth the same treatment, and it is
small -- but it is a want, not this week's need.

**My recommendation, to be overruled:** read the two numbers. If you are near the limit,
the honest question is whether the Standby Actor is earning its keep as a fifth MCP
surface when Bundle D and Smithery both point elsewhere. Downgrading it out of Standby is
cheaper than upgrading a plan for a surface nothing advertises.

**UNVERIFIED from the container:** apify.com, console.apify.com and api.apify.com are all
egress-blocked here, so I have not read your account state, your plan or your usage. The
mechanics above are from Apify's public documentation.

## Q3. Bundle B or OpenAI?

**Bundle B, clearly -- and the reason is a correction against me rather than a judgement
about the two.**

**I recorded Bundle B as gated behind the IAM split on 2026-09-15, and that was wrong.**
The scope doc says the catalog grant (`DescribeEntity`, `ListEntities`, `StartChangeSet`)
is an IAM change on `relayshield-breach-check-role-1sapnwdl` -- the role at 26 inline
policies, 10,127 of 10,240 bytes, 11 of 10 managed slots. That reads as *Bundle B waits
for a week of IAM work*.

**A change set is not a runtime operation, and that is the runtime role.** The fulfillment
Lambda needs `ResolveCustomer` and `MeterUsage` at runtime and has both.
`.github/workflows/marketplace_dimension.yml:64` assumes
`role/relayshield-github-deploy`, and that is the identity that calls `StartChangeSet`.

**The grant is already written, already targets the right role, and has never been run.**
`tools/apply_marketplace_catalog_policy.sh` builds one customer-managed policy from
`iam/relayshield-marketplace-catalog.json` and attaches it to `relayshield-github-deploy`,
then proves it with `simulate-principal-policy` against the role. Its header explains why
it is operator-side and once: a role cannot widen its own permissions, so the first grant
cannot happen inside Actions.

**So Bundle B's real remaining cost is small:** one read-only drift command, one grant
script, a change set that is already written and tested, then three small code edits
mirroring Bundle A. Against that, it is a revenue SKU on a shelf that already carries two
live products.

**Why OpenAI ranks below it despite the larger audience.** Step one is now partly read and
it turned up two gates rather than tasks:

- **Platform identity verification is a prerequisite** -- business verification to publish
  under RelayShield's name. That has its own latency and no amount of building shortens it.
- **Selling digital goods, subscriptions or in-app services is not yet allowed**, and the
  documented link-out route is scoped to physical goods.

**That second one is the Telegram Stars trap with another host's name on it.** We already
know that wiring "upgrade" from inside a Mini App to an external payment page is the route
that gets a bot restricted. A pay-per-call API sold from inside a ChatGPT app is the same
move. **The resolution is the same and it is already built**: list the KEYLESS checks,
which cost us nothing and sell nothing inside the host, and leave the paid rails outside
it. But that is a decision to take before writing any metadata, not after a rejection.

**So: Bundle B now. Start OpenAI's verification in parallel, because it is latency you
cannot compress and it costs one form.** That sequencing gets the revenue item moving
while the clock that gates the other one is already running.
