# Vouch: the partner-team contact, and why Joyce went quiet

*Researched 2026-08-14. Supersedes the Vouch section of `cyber_insurance_outreach_talking_points.md`.*

## The thing that explains the silence

**Vouch sold its underwriting arm.** On 6 August 2025 Hiscox agreed to acquire **Corix Insurance
Services, LLC** and **Vouch Insurance Company** from Vouch, Inc. Vouch, Inc. remains an independent
**broker**, focused on risk management services for technology, life sciences and professional
services, and has signed a multi-year distribution deal with Hiscox.

**This matters more than the choice of contact.** The pitch sent to Joyce on 2026-07-20 was an
underwriting-signal pitch: feed identity-compromise data into the carrier's own underwriting and
risk monitoring. **Vouch no longer owns an underwriting operation to feed.** That capability now sits
inside Hiscox.

So a referral to the perfect person inside Vouch would still land somewhere that cannot buy what was
offered. Chasing Joyce harder does not fix that, and neither does a better-written version of the
same message.

Joyce Calixto is an **Advisor Team Lead (Early Stage)**. She sold the policy and services the
account. A data-partnership ask was never in her remit, and it is even less so now.

## This splits into two different asks, at two different companies

### 1. Vouch, the broker. A partner-network ask, not an underwriting one.

Vouch runs a real, staffed partner program with three published categories:

| Category | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Venture Partners** | VCs helping portfolio companies get covered |
| **Technology Partners** | Integrating insurance into existing platforms and ecosystems |
| **Service Providers** | HR, legal, accounting, banking firms offering clients access |

Named partners include **Vanta, Carta, Brex, Ramp, Mercury, Y Combinator and AngelList**, 500+ in
total.

**Vanta is the tell.** A security and compliance product sitting in an insurance broker's partner
network is precisely RelayShield's shape. That is the slot to ask for, and it is a slot Vouch
actually staffs and markets.

**This contradicts the standing rule in `cyber_insurance_outreach_talking_points.md`** that says
never frame as a policyholder benefit, always as an underwriting signal. That rule was written for
**carriers doing continuous underwriting**, and it is still correct for Coalition, At-Bay and Cowbell.
It is the wrong rule for a broker whose partner program is explicitly distribution-shaped. Apply it
by buyer type, not universally.

### 2. Corix. The original pitch, correctly addressed, and WARM.

**Founder confirmed 2026-08-14: he is a Corix Insurance customer.** That single fact changes the
sequencing set out in the first version of this document.

The earlier recommendation was Vouch first, alone, on the reasoning that the policyholder
relationship was the only warm asset and that it existed only at Vouch. **That reasoning is now
wrong.** He holds a policy on Corix paper, so he has direct standing at both companies, which are
separating from each other.

**Send both, in parallel, as two different pitches.** They are separate companies with separate
buyers and there is no collision risk.

**Corix is the better opener of the two**, because "your own underwriting team priced my risk, and
here is a signal they did not have when they did it" is a sentence no cold vendor can write.

**Correction to the first version of this section: the acquisition has already closed.** I argued
there was a pre-close window worth beating. There is not. By June 2026 Corix is described as
"recently acquired" and Hiscox has appointed its own leader over it. The urgency argument was wrong
as stated.

**A better window replaced it, though.** See Lori Bailey below: she took the role in June 2026, which
is two months ago. A leader that new is materially more likely to take an outside conversation than
one settled three years into a remit, because she is still forming her view of what to change.

**Use the At-Bay-shaped underwriting-signal message for Corix**, not the partner-program message
below. Corix does dynamic underwriting for technology, life sciences, advanced manufacturing and
consulting, which is exactly the profile that pitch was written for.

**Do not mention the Hiscox acquisition in either message.** To Corix especially: the people reading
may not be free to discuss it, and raising it reads as presumptuous.

## The message

Do not mention Joyce, do not reference the unanswered pitch, and do not mention the Hiscox deal.
Naming their divestiture to a stranger reads as either due diligence theatre or an implied criticism.

```text
Subject: RelayShield, a Vouch policyholder, re: the technology partner program

Hi Sebastian,

I'm a Vouch policyholder. RelayShield has run Cyber and Tech E&O through Vouch since May 2026, and I'm writing about the technology partner side rather than my own policy.

RelayShield is identity-layer threat intelligence. We tell a company when its people's credentials, session cookies or OAuth tokens are circulating in criminal marketplaces and stealer logs, usually before it shows up in a public breach database. It is sold as an API and through AWS Marketplace, and the customers are early-stage technology companies, which is the same profile Vouch insures.

The reason I think there's a fit: every one of those companies gets asked about its security controls at renewal, and identity compromise is the part almost nobody can answer for. Vanta sitting in your partner network suggests you already see security tooling as adjacent rather than off-topic.

I'd be glad to make it available to Vouch policyholders on preferential terms, and I'm equally happy to point our own customers at Vouch, since they're companies that need exactly what you sell and mostly haven't bought it yet.

Is the technology partner program the right door for this, or is there a better one?

Andrew Gibbs
RelayShield
```

### Why it is built this way

**"I'm a Vouch policyholder" in the first line.** It is the only thing in the message that cannot be
said by a cold vendor, and it is the reason this gets read.

**"rather than my own policy"** stops it being routed back to an account rep, which is the failure
mode that produced the current silence.

**The offer is two-way.** Their partner program exists to generate distribution. A message that only
asks costs them effort and returns nothing. Offering to point customers at Vouch makes it a trade.

**"mostly haven't bought it yet" is deliberately honest.** RelayShield's customer base is small.
Claiming a large channel would be checkable and false, and the sentence is still true and still
attractive: uninsured early-stage tech companies are Vouch's growth market.

### The weak line, flagged by the founder 2026-08-14: "our own customers"

**He is right that it is the softest claim in the message.** Worth separating the two problems,
because they have different answers.

**Volume.** The honest customer base is roughly **one paying customer** (Bob Kramich, since
2026-05-26), **one comped power user** (Arjen, on a lifetime comp), a few dozen mostly free-tier API
keys, and about five active Telegram users. AWS Marketplace has **zero** paying customers. "Point our
own customers at Vouch" implies a referral stream that does not exist yet.

**Mechanism.** Vouch's published referral program is stated to be for **individuals, not business
entities**, with businesses routed to `hello@vouch.us`. So the specific thing offered may not even be
a structure available to a company. Promising a mechanism that does not exist is a worse error than
promising volume that does not exist.

**How bad is it, honestly?** Moderate, not severe. The sentence offers intent, not scale. It does not
claim a number, and a reasonable reader takes it as goodwill. The exposure is only if Clark builds on
it and asks what the volume is.

**Do not send an unprompted correction.** An unsolicited "actually we are tiny" email destroys more
conversations than it rescues, and nothing false was stated.

**If he does ask, answer with reach rather than customers.** That is the genuinely defensible asset:
rsscan on PyPI and Docker Hub as a free install, content syndicated across seven channels, public AWS
Marketplace listings, and the Discord and Telegram bots. The people who touch RelayShield are
early-stage technical companies, which is precisely Vouch's ICP. Lead with that, name the stage
plainly, and do not reach for a customer count.

**For any future version of this message**, replace the clause with something true about reach. For
example: *"Going the other way, the companies that find us are early-stage and technical, and most of
them are underinsured or not insured at all. I'd rather point them somewhere good than nowhere, if
there's a sensible way to do that."* That keeps the offer two-way, claims only what is true, and
leaves the structure for Vouch to propose, which sidesteps the individual-versus-business problem.

**The line that actually earns the reply is "I'm a Vouch policyholder", not the referral offer.**
That is worth remembering before optimising the wrong sentence.

**It ends on a routing question, not a meeting request.** "Is this the right door" is nearly free to
answer, and a redirect to the right person is a better outcome than a declined call.

## The message, to Lori Bailey

Written on the assumption she is already fluent in scan-based underwriting. **Do not explain the
model to her and do not name Corvus or Axis.** Referencing someone's CV back at them reads as
research theatre. Respect the background by pitching at its level instead.

```text
Subject: Identity-layer signal for Corix underwriting, from a Corix policyholder

Hi Lori,

I'm a Corix policyholder. RelayShield carries Cyber and Tech E&O on Corix paper, and I'm writing about the underwriting side rather than my own policy.

RelayShield is identity-layer threat intelligence. We detect when a company's credentials, active session cookies and OAuth tokens are circulating in infostealer logs and criminal marketplaces, usually ahead of the public breach databases.

The reason I think it's worth twenty minutes: scan-based cyber underwriting sees the outside of a company well, the attack surface, exposed services, misconfigurations. It cannot see whether the people inside it already have working credentials or live session material in criminal hands. Stolen session cookies in particular survive a password reset and bypass MFA, so they never appear in any control an insured can attest to on an application.

That's a different signal type from what continuous scanning collects rather than a competing one, and it moves on a different clock: it changes between renewals, not at them.

Would a short conversation be useful, either as an underwriting input or as portfolio monitoring on active policies? Happy to show what the data actually looks like on any domain you want to pick.

Andrew Gibbs
RelayShield
```

### Why it is built this way

**"I'm a Corix policyholder" first.** Same reason as the Vouch message. It is the only unfakeable
line available and it is what buys the next paragraph.

**"rather than my own policy"** keeps it out of the service queue.

**The third paragraph is the whole pitch** and it is deliberately specific. "Survives a password
reset and bypasses MFA" is the sentence that lands with someone who has priced cyber risk, because
it describes a loss that happens after the insured did everything the application asked.

**"between renewals, not at them"** is aimed at portfolio monitoring rather than new business. That
is the easier sale: it needs no change to the underwriting process, only a feed.

**The offer is a live demo on a domain she chooses**, which is falsifiable and therefore credible.

> **Fix the em-dash defect before sending this.** `/v1/metered/identity-risk-score` returns
> "Domain adobe.com scores 75/100 (F — CRITICAL)". If she picks a domain and takes the demo, that
> string is what a cyber insurance executive sees. It is a two-line fix in `relayshield_api.py`.

## What not to do

- **Do not chase Joyce.** She cannot action this, and a fourth message makes the next one easier to
  ignore.
- **Do not send the At-Bay message with the name changed.** That one is an underwriting-signal pitch
  and it is aimed at a capability Vouch has sold.
- **Do not mention pricing.** The ask is which door, not what it costs.
